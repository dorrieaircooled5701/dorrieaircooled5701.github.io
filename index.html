About

I work on how the Christian Bible came to be a book — and on what the physical habits of the scribes who copied it can still tell us about the theology of the people who made them.

My research sits where New Testament studies, papyrology and doctrinal theology meet. Two questions run through most of it. First: how did a set of apostolic writings become a canon, and what did the church think it was doing as that happened? Second: what do the material features of the earliest manuscripts — the contracted divine names, the ruled strokes, the codex format, the chapter divisions and other paratexts — reveal about early Christian devotion and belief?

Those questions have taken me through canon formation and canonical criticism, the nomina sacra, the rule of faith in Irenaeus and the second-century fathers, Pauline theology, and the paratextual tradition of the Greek New Testament. Alongside the historical work I have a continuing commitment to repairing the modern separation of biblical studies from systematic theology, which is the concern behind both the Scripture and Theology Forum and much of my editorial work.

I hold a ThD from Lund University and am based in Bergen, Norway.

Books

  • Den första Bibeln: Kanonbildningen och de tidigaste bibelmanuskriptens teologi Artos & Norma Bokförlag, 2026 · 202 pp. · due 14 September · ISBN 9789177773139

    What did the first Christians read, and can a text carry theology by means other than its words? The book asks why particular writings came to count as the New Testament, whether Scripture and canon are the same thing, and what makes a writing holy. Its argument is that the physical form of the emerging Bible mattered: Christians wrote in codices rather than scrolls, and marked the holiness of their texts from a very early stage. The manuscripts carry theological markers — nomina sacra, cross and creedal symbolism, elaborated titles and other paratextual details — which together form a fabric meaning more than the letters and words alone, and which point towards Christ and the Trinity in a dimension of the New Testament that usually goes unnoticed.

  • Christ the Center: How the Rule of Faith, the Nomina Sacra, and Numerical Patterns Shape the Canon Lexham Academic, Bellingham, WA, 2023 · 360 pp.

    An argument that the rule of faith is not a second-century overlay on Scripture but something already inscribed in the text as the scribes handed it on. The book traces the interlocking of canon, creed and the contracted divine names, and reads the numerical patterning of the nomina sacra as evidence of deliberate theological craft — evidence present in every early manuscript and absent from every modern critical edition.

  • Frihet och förtvivlan: Sören Kierkegaard om gudsförhållandet och självets helande Artos & Norma Bokförlag, Skellefteå, 2022 · 156 pp.

    An introduction, in Swedish, to Kierkegaard’s depth psychology and to the concept of freedom at the centre of his Christian existential thought. To be turned towards oneself, he writes, is freedom; its opposite is despair, the sickness of the self, with anxiety as its psychological counterpart. The book reads the God-relation as the ground on which Kierkegaard describes the self, and follows the movement he traces from despair towards healing and freedom.

  • Bibeln i funktion: Om kanon, kyrka och tradition FFG:s skriftserie 14 · BV-Förlag, with Församlingsfakulteten and Församlingsförlaget, Gothenburg, 2018 · 165 pp.

    A Swedish-language account of the Bible as a functioning canon within the life of the church, written for readers outside the guild: how the collection took shape, what tradition contributes to reading it, and why the question of canon remains a live one for congregations.

  • The Formation and Significance of the Christian Biblical Canon: A Study in Text, Ritual and Interpretation Bloomsbury T&T Clark, London, 2014

    A four-part account of canon formation that treats the canon as an act of literary preservation and actualisation of the church’s apostolic tradition — ‘the Scriptures and the Lord’ — addressed first to the church and then to the wider society. The study works through the linguistic and effective-historical, the textual and material, the performative, and the ideational dimensions of the canon in turn, giving sustained attention to the nomina sacra convention.

  • Mitä ovat apokryfikirjat? Apokryfit ja Raamatun kaanon Translated by Johanna Hartikainen · Kustannus Oy Uusi Tie, Helsinki, 2010 · 121 pp.

    Written for Finnish readers at a time when fresh translations of the Old Testament apocrypha were appearing, and taking up the questions those translations raise: what these books actually are, how they stand in relation to the biblical canon, and on what grounds the churches have answered so differently as to whether they belong within it.

T&T Clark Studies in Canon

Co-editor with Darian R. Lockett, book series

T&T Clark Studies in Canon — Bloomsbury T&T Clark

A new monograph series devoted to the formation, shape and continuing significance of the biblical canon. The series is open to work across the whole span of the question: the historical processes by which the Jewish and Christian canons took form, the manuscript and material evidence for those processes, canonical approaches to interpretation, and the theological and hermeneutical questions a canon raises for readers now.

Scripture and Theology Forum

Convenor and founding member

An international network for biblical scholars and systematic theologians

Biblical studies and systematic theology were, for most of their history, a single conversation. The modern division of labour between them brought real gains in specialisation and a real loss: theologians reading Scripture without exegetes, exegetes reading texts without doctrine. The Forum exists to put the two disciplines back in the same room.

The Forum brings together scholars of different professional and confessional backgrounds for conferences, colloquia and collaborative publication, including an open panel across five sessions that I have chaired. Its first major collective output was the volume Scripture and Theology: Historical and Systematic Perspectives (de Gruyter, 2023), which I edited with Ludger Jansen and Michael Borowski. A second volume, Science, Scripture and Theology: Moving Beyond the Impasse(s), is forthcoming from the same publisher.

Teaching and supervision

At NLA University College I teach New Testament exegesis and early Christianity across the undergraduate and graduate programmes, with courses on New Testament Text, Method and Interpretation, Matthew’s Gospel, Paul and his Letters, Johannine theology, Greek text reading, and a module titled Encountering God through Scripture. During my years at King’s College, Aberdeen, I taught New Testament in the School of Divinity, History and Philosophy and supervised research students in that field.

I supervise doctoral research at Union School of Theology in Wales, as part of its PhD supervisory team, where the programme can be taken from anywhere and supervision runs largely online. Nine doctoral students have so far completed their PhDs under my supervision. I welcome enquiries from prospective students working on canon formation, early Christian manuscripts and scribal practice, patristic exegesis, or the relation between biblical studies and systematic theology.
